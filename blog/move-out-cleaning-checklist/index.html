The Move-Out Cleaning Checklist NYC Landlords Actually Look For

26 November 2026·About 8 min read·CLEAN CLEANERS NYC

A move-out clean gets the apartment empty, tidy, and ready for a final walkthrough. The key to protecting your deposit is leaving it in the condition your lease requires, while documenting its condition and addressing any issues your landlord flags.

Short answer: Focus first on the oven and fridge interiors, empty cabinets and closets, bathroom grout, floors, and window tracks. Remove all belongings and trash, take dated photos when you're done, and check your lease for specific cleaning requirements. A clean apartment helps, but it can't guarantee your full deposit if there's damage or unpaid rent.

What NYC landlords and management companies check first

Every building is different, but these are the spots most likely to stand out during a final walkthrough:

  1. Oven and fridge interiors: Grease, food residue, crumbs, and spills are easy to spot.
  2. Cabinets, drawers, and closets: They should be empty and free of dust, crumbs, and forgotten items.
  3. Tub, shower, and grout: Soap scum, hair, and dirty grout can make a bathroom look neglected.
  4. Floors and baseboards: Check corners and edges, especially where furniture used to sit. Baseboards are easy to overlook.
  5. Window sills and tracks: Dust and debris collect in tracks, including on older pre-war windows.
  6. Walls and doors: Wipe off removable marks, but don't confuse cleaning with repairing holes or damaged paint.
  7. Leftover trash and odors: Remove everything from the apartment, including food, recycling, and items tucked under the sink.

If your lease or building has a move-out procedure, follow that too. New York security deposit rules generally allow deductions for certain unpaid charges or damage beyond ordinary wear and tear, not simply because a routine turnover clean is needed. Read the New York State law on residential security deposits and the NYC Bar's security deposit guide. If you receive deductions, ask for an itemized explanation.

Kitchen checklist

  • Empty and wipe inside every cabinet and drawer, including the space under the sink.
  • Clean inside the oven, including racks and door glass; wipe the drawer below if there is one.
  • Empty and wipe the fridge and freezer, including shelves, drawers, and door bins. Remove food and defrost the freezer if needed.
  • Wipe the microwave inside and out, plus the stovetop, knobs, and range hood.
  • Remove grease and food splashes from cabinet fronts, counters, and backsplash.
  • Scrub the sink and faucet, and clear crumbs or food from the drain area.
  • Sweep and mop the floor, including the edges and the areas where appliances stood.

If you can safely reach the space beside or behind an appliance, remove dust and debris. Don't force a heavy stove or fridge out of place, or disconnect an appliance to clean behind it.

Bathroom checklist

  • Scrub the tub or shower, tile, glass, and fixtures to remove soap scum and water marks.
  • Clean grout lines and clear hair from the drain.
  • Clean the toilet inside and out, including around the base and behind it.
  • Empty and wipe the vanity and medicine cabinet.
  • Clean the mirror, sink, faucet, and counter.
  • Dust the bathroom exhaust fan cover. Don't take it apart.
  • Sweep and mop the floor, including corners and the area around the toilet.

Bedrooms and living areas checklist

  • Remove all belongings, including items in built-in shelves and drawers.
  • Wipe doors, door frames, handles, light switches, and reachable light fixtures.
  • Dust radiator surfaces once they are cool. In pre-war apartments, radiators can collect a surprising amount of dust.
  • Remove surface dust and marks that wipe away safely. Don't scrub hard enough to damage paint.
  • Vacuum or sweep under furniture before it leaves, then clean the newly exposed floor.
  • Check walls for marks or damage. Cleaning may remove a scuff; patching holes or repairing paint is a separate job.

Closets and storage checklist

  • Empty every closet, shelf, drawer, and storage nook.
  • Wipe closet shelves and rods, and clean the floor.
  • Check high shelves and corners for dust, forgotten items, and hangers.
  • Remove shelf liners only if your lease or landlord asks you to; don't damage the surface.

Windows and walls checklist

  • Wipe interior window glass, sills, and reachable frames.
  • Vacuum or wipe out window tracks. They collect grit, especially in older apartments.
  • Dust blinds or shades if they're part of the apartment.
  • Check walls, doors, and trim for removable marks; note damage rather than trying an aggressive cleaner on painted surfaces.
  • Clean only areas you can safely reach from inside. Don't climb out a window or onto a fire escape to clean exterior glass.

Floors and baseboards checklist

  • Vacuum carpets and rugs that belong with the apartment, if required by your lease.
  • Sweep or vacuum hard floors, then mop with a surface-appropriate cleaner.
  • Clean edges, corners, and the floor beneath where furniture and boxes were.
  • Wipe baseboards throughout the apartment, including behind doors and around radiators.
  • Pick up visible debris and check for sticky spots or stains.

Shared spaces and entry checklist

  • Sweep or wipe the inside entry area and the floor just inside your apartment door.
  • Take all trash and recycling to the correct building area; don't leave bags in the hall or on the stoop.
  • Keep hallways, stairs, elevators, and building doors clear while moving.
  • Follow your building's elevator reservation, move-hour, and protection rules.
  • Don't leave belongings or cleaning supplies in a shared hallway or on a fire escape. Those are shared or safety areas, not extra storage.

DIY can work if you have time, supplies, and a manageable apartment. Plan for the appliance interiors, cabinets, bathroom, windows, floors, and all the small areas, not just visible surfaces. If you hire help, ask what the service includes and whether the apartment should be empty.

Move-out cleaning checklist: quick FAQs

Can my landlord charge me for cleaning?

It depends on your lease and the condition of the apartment. New York law generally distinguishes ordinary wear and tear from damage or conditions that may justify a deduction. Ask for an itemized statement if money is withheld, and consult the law or a tenant resource if you're unsure.

Do I need to clean if I hire movers?

Yes. Movers transport your belongings; they usually don't clean the apartment. If possible, schedule cleaning after the apartment is empty so floors, closets, and appliance interiors are accessible.

Do you clean empty apartments?

Yes. An empty apartment is ideal for a move-out clean. If you need service while boxes are still there, let us know when you book so we can plan around them.

Do you bring supplies?
